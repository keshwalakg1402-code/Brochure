Quantum Computing

Moving Beyond Bits to Qubits

Prepared by: Krish Keshwala | Class 10

Classical computers process information using standard bits (binary switches representing 0 or 1).


Quantum computers leverage quantum mechanics to evaluate massive datasets simultaneously, unlocking processing capabilities classical hardware can never match.

2. Core Physics Principles

  • The Qubit: Unlike a classic bit, a qubit can exist as 0, 1, or a superposition of both.
  • Superposition: Scales processing capacity exponentially with every added qubit.
  • Entanglement: Microscopic particles become linked; measuring one instantly reveals the state of another.
“Nature isn't classical, dammit, and if you want to make a simulation of nature, you'd better make it quantum mechanical.” — Richard Feynman

3. Key Applications

  • Drug Discovery: Simulating molecular structures to discover medicines in days.
  • Cryptography: Developing unhackable post-quantum security protocols.
  • Optimization: Instantly solving global supply chain and logistics models.

4. Technical Obstacles

  • Decoherence: Qubits lose quantum state from tiny temperature or electromagnetic changes.
  • Cryogenics: Requires operational environments near absolute zero (-273.15°C).

5. Conclusion

Quantum systems will soon solve challenges in minutes that classical supercomputers couldn't solve in millennia.
